Meet the machines

The hardware behind the missions

A lander touches down and stays put. A rover is a robotic vehicle that drives across another world to study its rocks, soil and surroundings.

Words to know
  • Sol: a Martian day, a little longer than an Earth day.
  • Spectrometer: an instrument that reads the light from a material to learn what it is made of.
  • APXS: a spectrometer that measures the chemical elements in rocks and soil.
  • RTG: a power source that turns heat from radioactive material into electricity.
  • Seismometer: an instrument that measures ground shaking.

The science behind what remains

Why old hardware still matters

Temperature extremes

Both worlds swing through very large day–night temperature changes, which stress metals, adhesives and electronics. Surveyor 3’s parts were returned in 1969 partly so engineers could examine such effects.

Radiation

Neither the Moon nor Mars has a protective global magnetic field like Earth’s, and Mars has only a thin atmosphere, so surfaces receive far more radiation than Earth’s.

Dust

Dust coated Surveyor 3 and later clung to Apollo gear. On Mars, dust built up on rover solar panels; wind cleaning events extended Spirit and Opportunity’s lives, while the 2018 global dust storm preceded Opportunity’s last contact.

Material degradation

Exposed polymers, coatings and wiring age differently in vacuum, UV and thermal cycling. Returned Surveyor 3 pieces are among the few spacecraft materials examined after time on another world.

How earlier missions helped

  • Surveyor landers demonstrated soft landing and surface survey before crewed Apollo landings.
  • Sojourner showed that wheeled rovers can work on Mars.
  • Spirit and Opportunity showed rovers can far outlast their planned lifetimes and find evidence of past water.

Legacy

What these machines taught humanity

Lunar exploration

Surveyor landers tested soft landings and the surface before Apollo crews went.

Robotic exploration

Sojourner showed a rover could drive on Mars; Spirit and Opportunity then worked far beyond their plans.

Planetary geology

Rovers read the history of a place from its rocks and minerals.

Past water

Spirit and Opportunity found evidence of water-related environments on ancient Mars.

Engineering

Dust, cold and power shortages shaped how later spacecraft were designed.

Later missions

The airbag landing system proven by Pathfinder was used again by Spirit and Opportunity.

The next generation of explorers

What the machines left us

Surveyor 3 parts brought home in 1969 let engineers study how hardware ages on the Moon. Rovers that outlasted their plans, and a mission that ended in dust, show how dust, power and long operation shape spacecraft design. Leaving equipment on other worlds also raises a question of responsibility: these sites are part of human history, and how to protect them is an open discussion.

We left machines behind. They left knowledge for us.
